Patient Graph: Plain-Language Guide

BRCA TCGA PanCancer Atlas 2018 — milestone 1

Main idea: the graph records one patient's clinical context and molecular observations from that patient's tumour sample. It is not yet a complete biological interaction network or a diagnosis model.

1. What is a feature?

A feature is one item of information, such as age, cancer stage, a mutation type, or a copy-number state. Features are usually stored as attributes on a node or edge; they are not automatically nodes.

Patient: age = 55, sex = Female, subtype = BRCA_LumA

We do not create separate nodes for values such as “Age 55” or “Female,” because they describe the patient rather than being independent biological entities.

4. Mutations and MAF

A mutation is a DNA sequence change. The project reads data_mutations.txt, a MAF (Mutation Annotation Format) table. Each row is one reported mutation record.

Term/fieldPlain meaning
Hugo_SymbolStandard short gene name, such as TP53.
Entrez_Gene_IdNumerical gene identifier used to reduce name ambiguity.
Variant_ClassificationBroad mutation effect: missense, nonsense, frameshift, intronic, etc.
Variant_TypeDNA change: SNP (one letter changed), insertion (letters added), or deletion (letters removed).
HGVSp_ShortShort protein effect, for example p.Arg175His.
Chromosome, position, allelesWhere the change was measured and its before/after DNA letter(s).
IMPACT, FILTER, NCALLERSBroad predicted consequence, quality status, and support from mutation-calling software.

A raw MAF-row count is not automatically the number of cancer-driving mutations, unique mutated genes, or tumour mutation burden.

5. CNA: copy-number alteration

Humans usually have two copies of most genes. Cancer cells can lose DNA or gain extra DNA copies. A CNA is this kind of change.

GISTIC valueMeaning
-2Homozygous deletion: both copies are estimated lost.
-1Hemizygous deletion: one copy is estimated lost.
0Neutral: no discrete gene-level CNA was called.
1Gain: extra copy/copies are estimated.
2High-level amplification: strongly increased copy number is estimated.

GISTIC is the dataset's computational method for converting copy-number measurements into these categories. Neutral does not mean a gene is unimportant or healthy; it means no CNA was called for that gene/sample.

Why neutral CNA calls are excluded: each sample has about 25,000 genes. If neutral calls became edges, the graph would mostly contain thousands of “no CNA” relationships. We retain -2, -1, 1, and 2 as alteration evidence. Raw zero values remain in the source dataset.

6. Why the example is a star graph

TCGA-A2-A0CO has one sample, TCGA-A2-A0CO-01. The sample is the source of each molecular fact, so it sits at the centre. This case has zero MAF mutation records and 5,496 retained non-neutral CNA gene calls: 1 Patient + 1 Sample + 5,496 Gene nodes = 5,498 nodes.

Patient → Sample → Gene A; Sample → Gene B; Sample → Gene C

The PNG intentionally draws only 30 genes. Purple arrows are CNA facts, black is the patient-to-sample link, and no red mutation arrows appear because this sample has no MAF mutation records. It is not yet a gene-interaction network; pathways, gene-gene interactions, diseases, and drugs belong to later milestones.

7. Why no Top-K method was used

We selected feature types based on meaning and data quality, not a predictive ranking algorithm. There is no trained diagnosis model, agreed target label, or single valid definition of “most important” yet. A Top-K list could mean frequent genes, largest CNA values, known breast-cancer genes, variance, or model importance.

The 30-gene image cap is only a display limit, not a biological Top-K policy. A future compact graph could explicitly retain high-level CNA events only, known breast-cancer genes, mutation-plus-CNA genes, or a documented Top-K score.

8. Data intentionally not used yet

  • Gene expression, methylation, proteomics, phosphoproteomics, and structural variants: separate high-dimensional data types deferred for later.
  • Continuous and arm-level CNA: alternate CNA representations that would duplicate evidence.
  • Survival fields such as OS, DFS, PFS, and DSS: outcomes excluded to prevent target leakage.
  • Race, ethnicity, ancestry, and technical collection metadata: not needed for the initial biological graph without a specific clinical/governance reason.
